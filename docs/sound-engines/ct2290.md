# The CT2290 measured (19 Oct 2026)

Michael's Creative SB16 CT2290 (DSP 4.13, A220 I5 D1 H5, set up by
UNISOUND /CS) on an Intel 430HX board with a PIIX3 and a Pentium MMX 233,
under JemmEx: every number here is [hw] for that one card, unless marked.
The same program then ran through uCDD (driver D7, `vcpi-children`, a disc
image mounted so the driver was engaged, the virtual SB16 on 5/1/5), and
in 86Box on its own SB16 (DSP 4.05) [trace]. Two runs each; the numbers
agree between runs to a few percent. The probe is HWPROBE (see
[methods.md](methods.md)); its timebase is the TSC calibrated on the BIOS
clock, so absolute rates carry about 0.01% of uncertainty (41h 22050
measured 22047.0 Hz).

"Virtual" below is uCDD's virtual SB16 on the same PC [hw]; a difference
between it and the bare card is a fault under rule 1 unless noted.

## Rates

| Programmed | CT2290 | Virtual SB16 | 86Box SB16 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 41h 22050 (B6h, B4h, C6h, C4h) | 22047.0 Hz | 22046 Hz | 22049.9 Hz |
| Time constant A5h (1Ch, nominal 10989) | **10845.7 Hz** | 11023 Hz | 10988.9 Hz |
| Time constant D3h (90h, nominal 22222) | **22047 Hz** | 22046 Hz | 22222 Hz |
| Time constant E9h (90h, nominal 43478) | **43383 Hz** | 44092 Hz | 43478 Hz |

The CT2290 does not play a time constant at 1000000 / (256 - TC): A5h is
1.3% slow, D3h lands on 22050, E9h is 0.2% slow. Three points do not give
the rule; a sweep of every time constant is the next measurement. The
virtual card plays A5h and E9h 1.6% fast (it takes them as 11025 and 44100)
and D3h right.

## Interrupts and the 8237 count

- **When a block's interrupt comes.** At every block end of B6h, B4h,
  C6h, C4h and 1Ch the 8237 count read in the handler sits exactly on the
  block boundary (0BFFh, 07FFh, ...): the interrupt comes as the DMA
  fetches the block's last byte or word. With the FIFO on, the first
  interrupt comes 0.71-0.73 ms (16 samples) earlier than with it off; with
  it off, about one sample before the period boundary. Virtual: the count
  is about 7Dh short of the boundary (125 bytes or words not yet moved),
  and FIFO on or off makes no consistent difference. 86Box: on the boundary, no FIFO.
- **Never acknowledged (M3).** C6h auto-init with 512-sample blocks and no
  2xEh read: one interrupt, and the count stops moving within about two
  blocks (5 distinct values in 30 reads over 300 ms). One acknowledge and
  it runs again (9 interrupts in the next 200 ms). Virtual: 7 interrupts
  in 300 ms and the count never stops. 86Box: one interrupt, the count
  never stops.
- **Short transfers**, from the command's last byte to the handler
  (medians):

  | Transfer | CT2290 | Virtual |
  |---|---|---|
  | 14h, 1 byte, TC A5h | 65 us | 689 us |
  | 14h, 12 bytes, TC D3h (DMX's priming) | 502 us | 775 us |
  | 14h, 64 bytes, TC A5h | 5.75 ms | 6.50 ms |
  | C0h, 1 byte, 22050 | 50 us | 675 us |
  | B0h, 1 sample, 22050 | 51 us | 679 us |
  | 80h silence, 1, TC A5h | 46 us | 775 us |
  | 91h, 64 bytes, TC E9h | 1.52 ms | 3.55 ms |
  | 14h, 1 byte, channel unmasked 20 ms after the command | 32 us after the unmask | 2.30 ms |

  The real DSP waits for its DMA (the last row) and finishes short
  transfers within a sample or so; the virtual card finishes them at its
  next output period.
- **F2h and F3h.** F2h to the handler: 36 us. Split: 36 us until mixer
  82h shows it, 2.1 us from there to the handler (JemmEx's reflection).
  F3h raises the 16-bit interrupt (82h bit 1) in 35 us. Virtual: 26 us
  with interrupts on; but **raised while the program has interrupts off,
  it reaches the handler 0.47-0.81 ms after STI** (2.1 us on the card).
- **The DMA count's steps.** During B6h and B4h at 22050 the count of
  channel 5 steps one word at a time, FIFO on or off (no bursts on this
  card and chipset); two reads of it agree at the first try. B6h mono at
  44100 Hz: the count never moved, in both runs that tried it (DMACNT R,
  JEMM boot and the stepped-aside CKIT run). Open.
- **Channel 5's address and page (M4).** The current address register
  moves with the transfer (address + count constant). Page register 8Bh
  read back what was written (00h: a weak test, page 0).
- **A 0Ch write between channel 5's two count bytes (M5)** changes
  nothing (64 of 64): the two controllers' flip-flops are separate.
  Virtual: the same.

## The mixer

- **80h and 81h are writable (M2).** 80h F2h, written F4h, read F4h,
  restored; 81h 22h, written 20h, read 20h, restored. The CT2290 is a
  software-configured card (UNISOUND /CS sets it, as Creative's DIAGNOSE
  did). Virtual: both read back unchanged. DigPak's write of 81h = 02h
  (B4) would change the real card's DMA selection.
- **83h has four bits (M1).** Written 0Bh, it reads FBh: bits 0-3 kept,
  bits 4-7 read 1 (FFh before). A 1-byte C0h transfer takes the same time
  with 0Bh in it. What the bits do is not known. Virtual: reads FFh
  whatever is written.
- 0Eh reads 00h (no SB Pro stereo switch), as measured on 17 Oct.

## FM

- Status: 388h 00h, 38Ah FFh, 2x0h 00h, 2x2h FFh, 2x8h 00h. The AdLib
  test reads 00h, C0h, 00h (OPL3 or a compatible: bits 1-2 clear).
- Timer 1 steps 80.469 us, timer 2 321.88 us: 14.318 MHz / 1152 and / 4608
  within 0.02%. 86Box: 80.000 and 320.000 us.
- Register writes: index and data written about 1.2 us apart (one ISA
  write each, no delay) lose the timer start in 8 of 8 tries; with one
  status read between writes (about 2.4 us apart) every write lands.
  86Box takes them at any spacing.

## MPU-401 (330h)

- Status at rest BFh. FFh is answered with FEh in 3.2 us (about 9.5 us
  when the interrupt handler runs meanwhile), 3Fh in 8-23 us. FFh in UART
  mode: no FEh. ACh and ADh: nothing (UART only).
- The acknowledge raises the card's interrupt (82h bit 2) 4.4 us after FFh
  and 9-18 us after 3Fh, in the second run; in the first run after boot no
  MPU-401 interrupt came at all. Open.
- MIDI out: the first byte is taken at once, the second about 25 us later,
  then one byte every 312.6 us: a holding register plus the shift
  register, at 2.3% above 31250 baud's 320 us a byte. 86Box takes a byte
  every 0.24 us. Through uCDD the port is the card's: the same as bare.

86Box charges 51 ns for the Sound Blaster's ports [trace]: a polling
loop that the real card makes take a millisecond takes 43 us there.

## What it means

- Confirmed on hardware: **F5** (the C-Dogs order plays on the CT2290 at
  once and after a 20 ms wait; under uCDD the count never moves).
- New faults, each against the CT2290 (rule 1): F7 time-constant rates,
  F8 the count at a block's interrupt, F9 a transfer never acknowledged,
  F10 an interrupt raised with interrupts off, F11 short transfers and
  late DMA, F12 mixer 80h, 81h and 83h, F13 the DSP's response times
  ([findings.md](findings.md)).
- For the emulators (86Box, DOSBox-X): the time-constant rates, the
  16-sample FIFO and the interrupt at the last fetch, the stop when not
  acknowledged, the FM timer period and write spacing, the MPU-401's line
  rate, and the Sound Blaster ports' access time.
